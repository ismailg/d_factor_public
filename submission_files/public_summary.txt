100-WORD PUBLIC SUMMARY FOR PRESS RELEASE

What makes some people exploit others' trust? We studied the "Dark Factor of Personality"—a core tendency to prioritize self-interest at others' expense. Participants played a money-sharing game with computerized partners that either behaved predictably or responded quickly to betrayal. Those high in this factor initially cooperated but gradually kept more money when partners remained trusting. However, when partners punished selfish behavior swiftly, even high-scorers restrained themselves. Interestingly, this strategic exploitation didn't pay off—exploitative players earned no more than cooperative ones. Our findings show that dark personality involves calculated social behavior that adapts to circumstances, not indiscriminate selfishness.
